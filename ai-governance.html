Governance Concerns — Magnificent 7 + SpaceX + Anthropic
Hand-compiled 25 Nov 2026 · framework: Guido Palazzo (HEC Lausanne), The Dark Pattern: The Hidden Dynamics of Corporate Scandals, interviewed in NZZ, 7 Nov 2026
Palazzo argues that big corporate scandals (VW diesel, Boeing 737 Max, Theranos, Credit Suisse, Enron) are rarely the work of a few bad people: they grow out of an organisational pattern in which people gradually stop seeing their own misconduct. He sees clear parallels in today's Silicon Valley and expects an Enron-type AI scandal in the near future. The pattern:
Rigid ideology — techno-utopianism ("AI will solve every problem"); critics and regulators become obstacles, rules seem not to apply. Like the dot-com "New Economy".
Toxic leadership — a climate of fear ("whoever has a problem is the problem", VW); nobody reports bad news upward.
Corrupting goals — targets everyone knows are unreachable; investor promises that keep growing and milestones that keep moving.
Incentives — pay cut loose from results (CS: $32bn bonuses in 2012–22 while losing $3bn and paying $11bn in fines).
Conformity — homogeneous, closed teams that see outside criticism as an attack ("a medieval castle with its cannons pointed outward").
Warning sign / trigger — many lawsuits and settlements across many units; the scandal breaks when the success story stalls.

Takeaway. Six of the nine are run by a founder with control or near-control (Alphabet, Meta, SpaceX by votes; Nvidia, Tesla, Amazon by influence), so the board is a weak brake. In Palazzo's view a dark pattern turns into a scandal when the success story stalls. For this group the stress test is the AI capex cycle: watch capex / cash flow, debt issuance and the circular deals in the card above.
